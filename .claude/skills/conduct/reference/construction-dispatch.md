# conduct — reference: Construction dispatch (ADR-049 workspaces; #553, #800, #853)

Part of the `conduct` skill — this section stood in `SKILL.md` and moved here VERBATIM (#1322), so a conductor's boot carries the protocol and opens this at the moment the duty applies. Nothing in it became optional by moving; `SKILL.md` names when to read it.

## The driver runs the ruled order (figaro relay R-736, his letter «A», 2026-10-02)

**A PROGRAM NOW RUNS THE RULED CONSTRUCTION ORDER OF ONE COMPONENT, AND STARTS A MODEL ONLY FOR CONTENT WORK.** The Software Architect approved the driver switch plan on figaro relay `R-736` (open item `OI-3496`) with its three recommendations, and added his own words. His whole turn, verbatim:

> «A / I expect that once the driver is in place, all conductor work is conducted using the driver. In case of tooling
> issues, they shall just be fixed. In case of issues with the „driver approach", halt the system and discuss with me.
> Further, the kaizen shall overlook the behavior under the driver and compare to how it behaved before so we can
> eventually evaluate the inprovement»

**His three rules, as the plan's addendum states them:** *"All conductor work runs through the driver once the driver is in place. A tooling issue is fixed. An issue with the driver approach halts the system and comes to you."* And: *"The Kaizen agent compares the work under the driver with the work before the switch."*

**THE ORDER DOES NOT CHANGE — THE DRIVER RUNS IT.** Everything below this section is still the ruled order and still binds: design check → spec tests → implement → land → **integration run** → cold analyzer read → **conformance audit ∥ craft review**, started together → **ONE fix round** answering both reports → the re-review (a comments-only fix gets the audit alone, `R-560` rule 5) → the stop rule of `R-282` (a flip-flop read from git, or five judging rounds). The bullets below are also what the driver's rendered briefs carry, and they are the recipe for every kind of work the driver does not run yet. A change to the order, to a reviewer's charter or to a gate is a question about the driver approach, never a driver setting: take it to him.

**TWO READINGS OF HIS WORDS — THE AI ARCHITECT'S, NOT HIS** (`OI-3496`). Cite them as readings:

- **(a) «all conductor work» is the target.** Each kind of work the driver cannot run yet — spec round, design check, devops act, architect act, UI — moves onto the driver as a TOOLING item, fixed and not escalated, one kind at a time. **Until its kind is covered, it runs by hand under the bullets below.**
- **(b) «halt the system» is a Kaizen cord pull**, by whoever sees an issue with the driver APPROACH: verdict variance, a driven review that passed a defect, or a route the ruled order does not cover. The line drains, nothing new starts, and the AI Architect opens an SA-ATTENTION session with him. A hard stop happens only if tokens burn or the tree is damaged (the goal's cord rule). **A tooling bug is not that: fix it** (file it in the toolchain tracker, fix it, run again).

**WHAT THE CONDUCTOR STOPS DOING — THREE THINGS, NOW THE DRIVER'S:**

1. **Writing the audit, craft and fix briefs by hand.** The driver renders them from facts on disk: the land, the implementor's report sections, the last audit's hand-across, the open carry rows for the files in the review range, and the craft review's questions for the audit.
2. **Starting an ops session for each land.** The driver calls the project's integration script, the one the chain plan names (`integration_script`, a path relative to the checkout; on Figaro `bin/integration-run.sh`). The script does the integration run, the cold analyzer read, the coverage handover and the record, and the driver reads its JSON verdict. A red run goes back to implementation, and no review runs on red.
3. **Filing carry rows at the walk.** The driver files each rule-6 carry a review sends to «the file's next change», as a comment on the open row that already names the file, or as a new row. A carry for a file the fix opens goes into that fix (Kaizen `K-183`).

The driver also persists the implementor's report and both review records beside the run record, and commits them. Those were conductor duties under the bullets below; under the driver they are its own acts.

**WHAT THE CONDUCTOR KEEPS — SIX THINGS:**

1. **The queue order.** Which component's chain starts next is still yours.
2. **The implementor's work order.** You write the round-1 implementor brief under the brief contract, and you write the chain plan from the design check.
3. **The planned extra steps.** A step of another role between the land and the integration run, such as a test-engineer micro-round, goes into the chain plan as `after_land`.
4. **Every «stop, ask».** The driver stops and names the reason in its summary. The stops are listed below.
5. **Each reviewer question for the architect.** The reviewers answer questions that need a ruling in `needs_ruling`, and the driver records them in the summary (`readings.needs_ruling`) and sends them nowhere. You route each one: a relay, or an open item.
6. **The retros the verb could not start.** The rule is `R-656` point 2 (`SKILL.md`, boot step 3): a conductor, or a session that failed, was refused or ran over its USD cap. At its end the chain dispatches the retros its own ended children owe through `theagent2 conduct retro`, batched as that verb takes them, then closes each retro and writes every subject's `mark-retro` (theagent-unchained #2066, the AI Architect's yes on figaro `OI-3606`). The summary lists them under `retros`. A retro `conduct retro` refused, or a mark that failed, is named in the verb's last lines and stays yours; `boot` lists it.

**The rest of the third-class walk below stays yours** (`warn` and `note` rows, lines addressed to `kaizen`). The plan moved only the carry rows to the driver.

**HOW A CHAIN STARTS.** The driver is the verb `theagent2 conduct drive` (theagent-unchained #2040; it replaced the Python prototype `experiments/conduct-driver/driver.py`). Write the chain plan (a JSON file; the prototype's `README.md`, «The chain plan — every field the driver reads», names every field, and its `plans/*.json` are past Figaro chains; the verb reads one optional field more, `tracker_repo`, the tracker's `owner/name`, by default the checkout's `origin`), then run, from the conducting checkout:

```
theagent2 conduct drive --plan <chain plan>
```

**A DESIGN CHECK RUNS UNDER THE VERB TOO, AS A CHAIN OF ITS OWN** (theagent-unchained #2048). You still write its brief by the design-check bullet below. In the brief, name both paths: the scratch path `.theagent-run/design-check/<date>-<slug>-design-check.md` and the final path `packets/runs/<Component>/<date>-<slug>-design-check.md`. Then write a plan of steps alone: `name`, `slug`, `component`, `component_name`, and `"steps": [{"kind": "design-check", "brief": "<the brief>", "record": "packets/runs/<Component>/<date>-<slug>-design-check.md", "cap": <USD>}]`, with no `implementor_brief`. Start it with the same `theagent2 conduct drive --plan <plan>`. The verb dispatches the `architect` with your brief as written. It reads the record from the scratch path and commits it verbatim to the final path, which was your act until now. Then it closes the chain. Its stops are the same as for every step: an open Kaizen pull, the hard limit at twice the cap, or no readable record (none at the scratch path, or one without `## For the conductor` and `## For the AI Architect`). The record's `## For the AI Architect` section, unless it says «None», becomes the summary's `needs_ruling`. You route it, and the test round waits until the gaps are ruled. The rest of the record is still yours to act on: the test round from its `## Test-brief lines`, and the next chain's implementor brief from its `## Implementor-brief lines`. The verb refuses a plan that carries a design check and the implementor round together, because the test round stands between them. Before the dispatch the verb also writes the component's open tracker rows into one file, `.theagent-run/design-check/<record name>.issues.md`, and appends one line naming it under your brief (#2070). Each row carries its labels, body and comments. The architect reads that file instead of opening the issues one by one. A later kind of work (an architect act, a devops act) is a new `kind` in the same `steps` list.

**A SPEC ROUND RUNS UNDER THE VERB TOO, AS A CHAIN OF ITS OWN** (theagent-unchained #2049). You still write the test brief by the test-first bullet below, from the design check's `## Test-brief lines`, after its `## For the AI Architect` gaps are ruled. In the brief, name the record's final path `packets/runs/<Component>/<date>-<slug>-test-spec.md`. Then write a plan of steps alone: `name`, `slug`, `component`, `component_name`, and `"steps": [{"kind": "spec-round", "brief": "<the test brief>", "record": "packets/runs/<Component>/<date>-<slug>-test-spec.md", "cap": <USD>}]`, with no `implementor_brief`. Start it with `theagent2 conduct drive --plan <plan>`. The verb dispatches the `test-engineer` with your brief exactly as written. The test engineer is the one `csharp/` writer for the round and commits its own cases and its own record, as before. The verb commits nothing for it except the chain's close record. **It keeps other writers out (figaro OI-3545).** It refuses to start while `csharp/` has uncommitted changes, or while another chain launched by the verb is live and writes `csharp/`. It checks both again at the dispatch. A chain made only of design-check steps is read-only and does not count as a writer. It does not see a writer you dispatched by hand, or a chain run with `--foreground` in a pane: keeping those out is still your job. It refuses a plan that also carries the implementor round, a design check, or a second spec round. The reasons: the implementor brief is written from this record; the test brief is written from the design check's record; and one chain has one `csharp/` writer. After the round it reads git and the record, and it stops, asking you, on the usual conditions: an open Kaizen pull, the hard limit at twice the cap, or no readable result. No readable result here means any of these: no committed record; a record without `## For the conductor`; a change under `csharp/` outside `csharp/Spec/`; or `csharp/` left with uncommitted changes. **The ADR-013 barrier:** the verb copies no text of the test record and no path the round changed into the summary, the close record or the journal. When the record's `## For the architect` is not «none», the summary's `needs_ruling` gets one line that points to the record. You route it from the record. Quoting `## For the implementor` (visible names only) into the next chain's implementor brief is still your act. A stopped spec round is not resumed over its own commits: its record already exists, so the verb refuses the plan. Read the record, then finish by hand or plan a new round with a new record name.

**The verb refuses in your turn, or launches and returns.** It refuses to start, exit 2 and one line per reason, while any lab-only part is reachable: a replay time, the lab's GitHub stand-in, a path under the lab's setup, or a missing integration script. It also refuses a plan whose `baseline_measure` names no measurement: measure the baseline first, at the commit round 1 starts from (`bin/integration-run.sh --measure-only <dir> --component C --path P`; the README gives the whole recipe). Otherwise it starts the chain in a tmux session of its own, `drive-<label>` (the label is `<plan name>-real` unless `--label` names another), and your turn goes on.

**The chain does not die with your pane.** The tmux session belongs to the tmux server, not to your session, so a succession can happen while a chain runs. Its progress is the journal `.theagent-run/driver/journal-<label>.jsonl`. When it ends, its exit code is in `.theagent-run/driver/drive-<label>.done`: 0 means closed, 3 means stopped and asking, any other code is a fault of the verb. Its summary is in `.theagent-run/driver/summary-<label>.json`, and the close record is committed beside the run record. The children it dispatches are ledger rows like any other, and each wakes the conducting pane when it ends. A successor finds a running chain with `tmux ls` or its journal. It continues a stopped chain with `theagent2 conduct drive --plan <chain plan> --resume .theagent-run/driver/summary-<label>.json`. `--foreground` runs the loop in your own pane instead, and then the chain dies with that pane.

**WHAT THE DRIVER ENFORCES ON EVERY LIVE CHAIN:**

- **Nothing new starts while a Kaizen pull is open.** Before every dispatch (the implementor, a planned step, the review pair as one step) the driver reads `theagent2 kaizen list --open`. It reads the book again before the land of an implementor round that has ended, and before that round's integration run. This is the AI Architect's reading of the drain (figaro `OI-3504`), not the Software Architect's words; it cites Kaizen `K-057`: *«the DRAIN holds work. From 22:07Z the construction line held: S-1874's own land step and integration run»*. With one pull open, or with a book it cannot read, the driver stops the chain at that point: no new child, no land, no integration run. A child already running is not killed: it finishes its current piece, and the summary names it in `running_at_stop`.
- **The hard limit is TWICE the child's USD cap** (`R-736` Q1, letter «B»: *"A hard limit at twice the cap. Only a runaway session ends."*). The plan, in its own words: *"The cap warning of R-728 stays."* A child that ends at the limit carries no verdict, and nothing it committed is landed: the driver stops and asks.
- **The chain readings** (`R-736` Q2, letter «A»): `readings` in the summary and in the committed `<run>.close.md` record the cost, the rounds, the time from the first land to the close, and every stop.

**WHEN THE DRIVER STOPS, YOU HANDLE IT:**

| the summary says | what you do |
|---|---|
| `held: Kaizen cord pulled … before <the act>` | the line drains. Collect each child in `running_at_stop` (`theagent2 conduct await <id> --close`) and start nothing new. Once the pull is ruled, continue the chain. Held before the land of a round, its workspace waits as the implementor left it: land it and run the rest of the chain by hand under the bullets below. Held before the integration run or the review pair of the first round, `--resume <summary>` reuses that round's land. A later round runs by hand under the bullets below until the verb covers it |
| `held … could not be read` | a tooling issue: fix it, then continue as above |
| `stop, ask (decider/SA): …` (a refused act, an implementor that committed nothing, no readable verdict, a FAIL with no `fix-now` row) | decide whether the ruled order covers the case. If it does and the driver erred, that is a tooling issue: fix it and run the rest by hand. If the order does not cover it, that is the driver approach: pull the cord (reading (b)) |
| a child `ended at its hard limit` | no verdict exists. The ended session owes a retro (over budget, `R-656`); re-dispatch only after you know why it ran away |
| `flip-flop (R-282 «A»)` or `five judging rounds` | unchanged: that component halts, and an SA-ATTENTION session carries the commits. The other components keep building |

**THE KAIZEN AGENT COMPARES BEFORE AND AFTER** — his words: *"the kaizen shall overlook the behavior under the driver and compare to how it behaved before so we can eventually evaluate the inprovement"*. The Kaizen agent records each chain's readings (`R-736` Q2, letter «A»). It pulls the cord when a later stage finds a defect that a driven review passed. Readings for the hand-conducted chains before the switch are in `experiments/conduct-driver/RESULTS.md` (cost and minutes from land to close, per chain).

## The ruled order, bullet by bullet — the driver runs it, and a kind it does not run yet runs by hand from here

These three bullets stood under *Spawn recipes* in `SKILL.md`.

- **Construction dispatch (materialized ADR-049 workspace):** a workspace session is a
  conducted session like any other, and `workspace materialize` now wires it as one — the
  ADR-056 §6 hooks go into the workspace's own `.claude/`, pointed at THIS repo's conduct
  surface (`THEAGENT2_CONDUCT_ROOT`, which the materializer writes). So: materialize FIRST,
  then `theagent2 conduct dispatch --brief <file.md> --role implementor --node construction
  --name <work-slug> --cwd <workspace>`, then `close` — the same verb as every other stage
  session, so the brief is linted, retained at `.theagent/conduct/briefs/<id>.md` and
  committed **in this repo** before the launch; the workspace gets the brief's text as its
  prompt and no stray file (`workspace preflight --phase resumed` would call one a dirty
  tree). Run 4 hand-typed this launch instead, and both Construction-stage briefs — the only record of
  what the implementors were shown, and so the only evidence the ADR-013 barrier held —
  exist nowhere on disk (#829). The verb also carries the entry id in, which hand-typing
  lost too: launch without it and the implementor runs unbound — no session id, no
  transcript, no metrics row, no handover retro, no andon, the way Run 4 lost both Construction-stage
  implementors and $22.66 of construction cost (#828). The materialize banner's `conduct`
  line tells you whether this repo is conducting a run at all.
- **"Every write was refused" is a path defect, not a concurrency defect** (#553). It was
  filed as concurrent-launch interference and that was disproved three times — including two
  probe rounds that dispatched concurrently and wrote fine. The real cause both times was a
  path resolved against a foreign root. Two things now catch it for you: the brief lint above,
  and `conduct boot`, which scans each headless dispatch log and prints `WRITE REFUSALS in
  that log` next to the completion — the failure is otherwise invisible, because such a child
  exits **0** and reports in detail on work it never wrote down. When you see that line, check
  what actually landed (`git log` in the workspace) before you believe the report.
- **One component per construction dispatch — always** (ADR-049 §8b, invariant 5; SA ruling
  2026-08-21, #800). If the work in front of you spans two components, it is not one dispatch
  to place; it is a design gap to route. A contract or seam change that breaks callers belongs
  to the architect: change the model, regenerate the contract surface, THEN dispatch each
  affected component separately to repair its own hand-written code. Never dispatch an
  implementor into this repo or into a clone because the work "would not fit a packet" — that
  the work does not fit is the signal, and the tree it would be sent to refuses it correctly
  (`workspace preflight` exit 3). Run 4 did exactly this for eight minutes and taught a session
  that a REFUSE is negotiable. Land the repairs with the coordinated land-back (#853):
  materialize every affected component off ONE explicit `--baseline <sha>` (HEAD moves with
  each run-record commit, so HEAD is the wrong spelling for the second member), then
  `theagent2 workspace land --group <ws…> --verify '<build command>'` — the members are
  applied together to a scratch worktree of HEAD, the verify command must come back green
  there, and only then does the repo receive ONE commit carrying every member. A red verify
  (or a tampered, empty, or off-baseline member) refuses the whole set with the tree
  untouched, so there is no red window to carry; sequencing single land-backs for a spanning
  change is now the anti-pattern.

- **FIRST LIGHT BEFORE THE WAVE — A TIER OF SEAM-BEARING COMPONENTS IS NOT DISPATCHED UNTIL ONE OF THEM HAS RUN ONE SEEDED CASE THROUGH ITS SIMULATOR AT ISOLATED WIRING.** The Software Architect ordered it on relay R-260, 2026-09-13 — *"please address all recommended measures."* — as measure **P3** of the five-whys analysis of this run's first Access build round: four Access components dispatched as one tier, **all four stopped on design gaps**, and two of the five gap kinds (*"the answer has no source"*, *"no store call can do the job"*) cannot be seen by any document review — they appear the moment real code meets a real simulator. The measure in its own words: *"One Access, one seeded case, through its simulator at isolated wiring, before the tier is dispatched."* **One component pays for the whole tier's lesson**, so build it, land it, light it, and only then place the rest of the wave. **It is a condition of the construction-entry check, not a promise you make in the wave plan:** the check reports `[S9] first light` on every run (`just construction-entry-check` in this checkout; the project's own recipe forwards flags to `construction-entry-check.mjs`), and `--require-first-light [<Access|seam>,…]` — no value for *at least one seam of the run*, names for *the component you are about to dispatch* — turns an unlit run red. **What it reads is the spec runner's own output, never a record anybody types:** with `THEAGENT_SPEC_RESULTS=<dir> THEAGENT_SPEC_WIRING=isolated` set over the spec assembly (`just spec-verdict` exports both), the run writes `<assembly>.isolated.results.json`, and the check looks in it for ONE green `protocol: <Seam> …` case — the ADR-059 derived durable round-trip, which writes and reads back through the Access's own contract operations against the registered simulator. A tier whose first component is lit is a tier whose seam, sheet, simulator and contract have been shown to compose once; a tier dispatched before that is four sessions discovering the same gap in parallel, which is what this run paid for.

- **FOR A NEW OPERATION THE TEST IS WRITTEN FIRST — THE SPEC-TEST ROUND RUNS AFTER THE CONTRACT LANDS AND BEFORE THE IMPLEMENTOR ROUND.** The Software Architect ruled it on relay `R-322`, 2026-09-15, his letter «A», and it **CHANGES HIS OWN RULED ORDER of 2026-09-13** (`R-245`), the order the craft-review bullet below still spells. His words, whole, as they stood on his screen:

  > **A — write the test first.** The test round runs after the contract lands and before the implementor
  > round. The implementor then sees a failing test and makes it pass. This adds no session, because the
  > test round is owed anyway. Risk: implementation can still change the contract, and the test is then
  > rewritten.

  Four clauses, every one from his screen, and all four bind you:

  - **THE TEST ROUND MOVES AHEAD OF THE IMPLEMENTOR ROUND FOR A NEW OPERATION**, after the contract lands. So the order for a NEW operation read: contract lands → **spec tests** → implement → land back → integration run on `main` at the widest wiring available → cold analyzer read → conformance audit → craft review. Nothing after the implementor round moved under `R-322`. **Since `R-560` (ruled into this playbook on `R-633`, letter «B», 2026-09-29) the line reads: contract lands → DESIGN CHECK → spec tests → implement → land back → integration run → cold analyzer read → CONFORMANCE AUDIT ∥ CRAFT REVIEW, started together** — the design-check bullet below and the parallel-review bullet further down.
  - **THE IMPLEMENTOR STARTS FROM A FAILING TEST AND MAKES IT PASS** — his words, *"The implementor then sees a failing test and makes it pass."* A red spec case is therefore the implementor's STARTING CONDITION for a new operation, not a defect it reports; say so in the brief, and name the case.
  - **IT ADDS NO SESSION: A SESSION MOVES, NONE IS BOUGHT** — his own ground, *"This adds no session, because the test round is owed anyway."* **State this wherever you plan a wave.** A reader who thinks the run just bought a round per operation will route around the rule on cost, and the cost is not there: it is the same `test-engineer` dispatch that was owed after the land, placed earlier in the same wave.
  - **HE ACCEPTED THE RISK, IN THESE WORDS:** *"Risk: implementation can still change the contract, and the test is then rewritten."* **No mitigation was offered to him and none may be invented** — not here, not in a brief. When implementation changes the contract, the test is rewritten; that is the ruled outcome, not a failure to escalate.

  **HE RULED AGAINST THE CHEAPEST READING KNOWINGLY.** The one closed precedent — the ERP component whose late test passed at once — stood on the same screen in its own paragraph.

  **WHAT THIS DOES NOT RULE, AND WHAT YOU MUST THEREFORE NOT INFER — NOT HERE AND NOT IN A BRIEF: who dispatches the test round; how a NEW operation is told from a changed one; and what happens when the contract will not compile yet.** Those three are open execution questions. They are named here so that nobody reads this bullet's silence as an answer; a file that answers them quietly has legislated past him.

  **AND WHAT IT LEAVES STANDING:** the rest of `R-245`; the `R-276` letter «D» terminator below (its stop rule is `R-282`'s, not the round counter the option text carried); the ADR-013 barrier, which does not move, so **the test stays the test engineer's to author**; and every component already landed — **no landed component is re-opened and `figaro#876` is unchanged.**

- **BEFORE EACH TEST ROUND OF A COMPONENT OR A SCREEN, ONE ARCHITECT DESIGN SESSION — THE DESIGN CHECK.** The Software Architect ruled it on relay `R-560`, 2026-09-26, his letter «A», answering *"Which one change do we test first on the next component or screen, so that less work comes back?"* He ruled it into the golden process on relay `R-633`, his letter «B», 2026-09-29, over a recommendation to keep it in one run. **No measurement confirms this step yet, and he chose it knowing that:** the option he selected said so in as many words, and on the run that raised it the two readings of its trial (Kaizen `K-174`) covered four of nineteen checks and both read `inconclusive`. What it binds you to, and it is all of it:

  - **DISPATCH ONE `architect` SESSION BEFORE EACH TEST ROUND OF A COMPONENT OR A SCREEN. THE TEST ROUND STARTS ONLY AFTER THAT SESSION'S GAPS ARE RULED.**
  - **THE ARCHITECT CHANGES NO CODE.** It writes three lists into its record and runs one compile: (1) every state the contract can express, against the drawn path that handles it; (2) every drawing sentence about a screen, against the screen element that shows it; (3) every caller of a shared service, against the service operation it calls. **The compile:** build the test project against the final contract before any test is written. `agents/architect.md` carries the session's side of this; name the component, the test round it precedes and the record's destination in the brief.
  - **EACH GAP IS RULED BEFORE THE TEST ROUND** — by the architect where the decision is the architect's, and put to the Software Architect where it is his. A list with no gap says so, and that is a valid result.
  - **SCOPE:** the three lists cover what the test round will test — its operations, its drawings, its screen elements — not the whole model. That is the reading of the Kaizen session that first installed the rule, not his words; a conductor who reads the ruling more widely follows the ruling.
  - **THE RECORD LIVES BESIDE THE COMPONENT'S RUN RECORDS**, as `packets/runs/<Component>/<date>-<slug>-design-check.md` (on the run that raised it, the architect wrote it to scratch and the conductor committed it there; under the verb, `theagent2 conduct drive` commits it — the paragraph under «HOW A CHAIN STARTS»). **A comment that cites one of its rulings cites that path plus the ruling id, and the implementor brief carries that exact string:** `Design: packets/runs/<Component>/<record>-design-check.md M3` — never «the round-10 design check», never a session id, never a bare ruling id. Take the path from `git ls-files packets/runs | grep design-check`. `packets/runs/` is in no implementor workspace, so the implementor can neither read nor grep the record, and the brief is the only place that string can come from (Kaizen `K-182`: over nineteen checks, ten implementor reports carried an open line about it, and one audit failed four citations written in prose).
  - **Why this step exists, once:** on the run that raised it, gaps in drawings, contracts and specs that a LATER step found were the largest single rework class (Figaro `.theagent/notes/2026-09-26_where-the-time-went-since-the-speech.md`, sections 3 and 6).

- **AND THE IMPLEMENTOR'S OWN REPORT IS A CONDUCTOR DUTY TOO — IT IS THE ONE REPORT THE SESSION THAT WROTE IT CANNOT FILE.** Its report is its FINAL MESSAGE; persist it beside the run record as `packets/runs/<Component>/<sha7>-rN.report.md`, verbatim, under a header naming the session, the workspace provenance commit and the land sha. The implementor lives in a materialized workspace, `packets/` is in the conducting checkout, and the brief lint (#553) refuses any path outside the workspace — so the file exists only if YOU transcribe it.
