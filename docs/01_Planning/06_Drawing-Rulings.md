# Bearing v2 — Drawing Rulings

> **What this is.** The rulings made while reviewing BP01 and BP02 about **how a business process
> is drawn** — what earns a box, what earns a call site, what belongs to the Detail Design instead.
> They are **not business rules**: they constrain the drawing, not the system's behaviour. The
> business rules are [`01_Intent_Gate-Document.md`](01_Intent_Gate-Document.md) §4, the surface
> decisions [`05_UX-Directives.md`](05_UX-Directives.md).
>
> **Why collected.** Each was ruled once, on one process, and holds for every process whose
> drawing raises the same question — so they are applied in one sweep instead of re-discovered one
> review at a time.
>
> **Grouped by status** against BP03…BP19 as they stand today. A ruling a program can decide
> belongs in `bpmn-dsl lint`; one only a reader can hold belongs in the
> `review-business-process-bpmn` skill. This page is retired when every row has gone to one or the
> other.

**Citations.** `SA <date>` is a direct Software Architect decision; `BR-…` and `UX-…` cite Intent
§4 and the UX directives; `lint <n>` is a rule `bpmn-dsl lint` already enforces.

| DRW-29 | A seam — a gesture the surface starts and another process owns — is a branch of the hub like any other act: one step that asks the system for nothing, a note **naming the process** whose business it is, and an end. A sub-process reached on the surface the user is already on takes the same shape (BP05's measures → BP06). It mints no call site. An act on the surface's **own item** is not a seam, even where another process carries on with what it started: it draws the request it sends and mints this process's own call site. Three stopped being seams on 2026-10-16 — *check out a goal*, which reads the statuses the system infers for it (`BP05.18`) and leaves the sitting to `BP07`; and the goal and the project defined on an idea or on a goal, whose editor opens over this surface and is **submitted here** (`BP04.11`, `BP04.12`, `BP05.19`), the owning process keeping what follows. What stays a seam is a move to another **surface**, where that surface's own drawing begins. The line is what the surface's own read already carries: an act on a sub-item the read brings along — everything BP06 does to a progress measure, checking in included — is that entity's process and is NOT restated here, or the hub would carry the other hub twice; a seam is for a gesture that is a whole other flow on a surface of its own. Two surfaces have no hub to carry one: the advisor, reachable from every tab, and a linear drawing like BP08. **Where the process behind the seam would otherwise open itself, the seam carries two steps** — the act that opens the surface, and the handover into the process that works it — and that process then starts inside the open surface, on its own hub: an opening drawn on both sides puts one act in two processes (SA 2026-10-18). | DRW-02, SA 2026-10-16, SA 2026-10-18 | Eleven remain, every one of them a step with a note naming the process it hands over to and an id in its own label — BP04 one, BP05 three, BP06 one, BP11 four, BP17 two. BP13 is the other candidate and is **not** done: its opening box also carries the request that computes the account, so it is not a pure opening. ## Unknown — no program can decide these

*Nothing is needed from you. These need a READER, not a ruling: the blind pre-review reads each drawing against this row before you see it, and a finding reaches you as one question about that process — never as a row to adjudicate here.*

| # | Ruling | Why | Why it cannot be checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRW-01 | A technicality is one step, never a sequence. Which preconditions are checked, how a path is resolved, how a folder is brought under version control: one box, and the detail belongs to the Detail Design. | Listing technicalities in a business intent is not acceptable; the stakeholder reads what the system is asked for, not how it complies. | Whether a box is a technicality is a judgement about its subject |
| DRW-02 | A step that asks the system for nothing is drawn only where it is significant from the user's point of view. The act a request leaves from and the seam into another process are significant; showing an editor, expanding or collapsing a section and folding a panel are not, and which surface it is lives in Intent §7. | What the drawing owes the reader is the user's path, not the UI's doings — but a path with its significant acts left out is not the user's path either. | Significance is the reader's judgement. A verb sweep found no insignificant step drawn; a step with any other wording is invisible to it |
| DRW-08 | A drawn note says only what the boxes, the gateways and the bound rules do not already say, and never restates what the Intent document states. Referencing Intent is fine, and the reference is the bound rule on the step. Provenance and remarks about the model are source comments. | A note is read by the stakeholder at the drawing; everything else is read by the next author at the source, and a rule the drawing has to lean on is already drawn as a bound rule. | `lint 5` catches provenance only; redundancy against Intent needs a reader |
| DRW-10 | Derivable does not mean transient. A fact is transient when it can be derived again whenever it is needed: nothing records it, nothing clears it, and it is drawn as the earlier answer read again, asked once the shared tail converges. A fact is persistent when it could not be derived again — its inputs move on, or it is a human judgement that merely coincides with a derivation — and then the step that records it is drawn. How a transient fact travels inside its chain is Detail Design. | A first run is the launch on which a folder had to be set up and ends with it; a check-out's log is derived from a board that then moves on, so it is recorded; an activity's Done is the user's judgement even when every task is done. | Whether the inputs survive is a fact about the data, not the drawing |
| DRW-14 | **No process draws a page read.** The product loads what it keeps at start-up and derives from it everything a surface shows — counts, flags, orderings, what is due, what is ready — so arriving on a surface asks the system for nothing and is a user-interface event (`UIP01`). A chain survives only where the system **observes something for a judgment**: the statuses inferred at a goal's check-out, the account a work check-out is settled against. | Counting a theme's goals is not an act the user performs, and when data is read is a detail design decision, ruled to happen once at start-up (SA 2026-10-17). What is kept is Intent §3 and the record inventory; what is computed, with the rule behind each value, is the derived-data inventory. | Whether a chain observes for a judgment or just shows a surface is a judgement about the act |
| DRW-19 | A cascade below a taken descendant is that descendant's own rule, applied where the act reaches it, and is not redrawn. The same holds wherever one process runs another's operation: BP16 defers to whatever that operation discloses rather than drawing it again. | The disclosure reaches as far as the rules do without this drawing restating another entity's cascade, and each cascade has exactly one drawing — BP02 reports the goal cascade's reach, BP05 draws it. | Requires reading the descendant's rules against this drawing |
