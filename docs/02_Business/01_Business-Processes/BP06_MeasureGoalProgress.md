# BP06 — Measure a goal's progress

**Stage.** Discovery / Mockup — before the Intent gate. Nothing here is const.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | **User** — at the start of a cycle to say what counts, then on their own out-of-band cadence to record where it stands, and whenever one turns out not to say anything about the goal. |
| **Goal** | A goal carries the typed measures it is judged against, each tracking a current value against its target, with a dated, append-only history of where it stood and the user's own notes — and a measure that does not work for the goal is set aside with its history, or dropped. |

## The flow in one paragraph

This is the whole life of a goal's progress measures, reached from the Goals view where BP05 hands it over as an act of that surface. It has no overview of its own: a measure is a sub-item of its goal with no page of its own, so the measures arrive with the goals on the Goals view (`BP05.01`) — the active ones, an archived measure being reached through the archive instead (`BP15`) — and this process acts on what is already there. An archived goal is never among them — the Goals view carries the active goals only (BR-87) — and BR-05's freeze is held by each write's own verification (BR-128). One act follows: say what counts, change what counts, reorder the measures, set one aside, drop one, record where one stands, or open what was set aside. Looking at how a measure has moved is no act of its own: its check-ins are in play with it, in date and time order, so looking writes nothing and decides nothing. Each is one path and ends on what the gesture did; that the measures stay in front of the user and the next act is available is the work loop every surface has, stated once in the path patterns rather than drawn as a return edge per act. A measure is typed numeric or boolean and there is no untyped measure. Numeric carries a start value, a target, a unit and a direction, with the current value initialized to the start and moved by each check-in — tracking that value against the target is the whole point of a measure — and boolean carries done and not done, starting at not done. The type is amendable afterwards like the start value, the target and the unit, and the check-ins already recorded are left as they stand, each reading in the type it was recorded under. Nothing records that the type changed: a measure has no change history, and that an operation happened is the operation log's business — every user operation is logged, so no drawing models it. Setting a measure aside is how one that turns out not to say anything about the goal leaves the working set without being destroyed, and dropping it destroys it instead. Neither act discloses anything and neither is approved, because the only thing a measure owns is its check-in history, which goes with it unasked and comes back with it: a project parented on it keeps its parent where the measure is archived, and survives minus the link where it is dropped (BR-96, BR-115). An archived measure comes back only into a live goal (BR-25), and the way to it is drawn: opening the archived measures is one step and a note, the Goals archive listing each one under the goal it belongs to, and reading, unarchiving and destroying it are `BP15`'s (BR-26, DRW-29). Archiving the goal itself still takes its live measures and their history along unasked, which is BP05's act and not this one's. A check-in asks for nothing first. The measures and where each one stands are in play with the goals (`BP05.01`), loaded once at start-up like every other record (DRW-14): the user fills the check-in in and submits it — the two boxes every composed write is drawn with, because what is typed is a draft until it is committed (BR-121) — and that submit is what moves the current value. The check-in history is in play with the measure as well, so looking at how one has moved is an act that asks the system for nothing — the dated points in date and time order, writing nothing and deciding nothing; whether a check-in form also shows that trend is the UI's business. A check-in is one dated data point with an optional note, and the history being append-only is what decides a second check-in on an already-covered date — another data point beside the first, never a correction. Nothing is inferred and nothing is judged here: the per-measure status is the check-out's business (BP07).

## Call sites

Five, each one synchronous call into the system. **`BP06.01` *Read a measure's check-in history* is retired on 2026-10-17**, and its id is never reused (ADR-066): every record is loaded at start-up, so the check-ins are in play with the measure and looking at how one has moved asks the system for nothing (DRW-14). **`BP06.11` *Archive a progress measure* is minted on 2026-10-16**, with the ruling that a progress measure is archivable on its own (BR-23). **`BP06.06` *See what removing a measure takes* and `BP06.10` *See what archiving a measure takes* are retired**, and their ids are never reused (ADR-066): the only thing a measure owns is its check-in history, which goes with it unasked, so neither act discloses anything and neither is approved — a project parented on it is never destroyed either way, which is the shape a task's deletion already has (BR-17, BR-115, SA 2026-10-16). **`BP06.08` *Read a goal's progress measures* is retired**, and its id is never reused (ADR-066): a goal's measures are its own sub-items with no page of their own, so they are read with the goals in `BP05.01` and this process acts on what the Goals view already carries (SA 2026-10-14). **`BP06.04` *Amend a progress measure* is retired**, and its id is never reused (ADR-066): one editor per entity serves creating and editing alike, and nothing separated the two chains but the fields they stored, so `BP06.03` *Edit a progress measure* is the one editing request (UX-04, SA 2026-10-11). **`BP06.09` *Draft the measure input* is retired**, and its id is never reused (ADR-066): every draftable field is kept by **`BP19.01`**, drawn once in `BP19 — Keep an edit as a draft`, and this process binds **BR-121** to the step that fills the field instead (SA 2026-10-11). The ids are **call sites of this process**
(`BP06.yy`, ADR-069) — which System Interaction each resolves to is a Design decision, recorded in
`si-binding.yaml`, never a rename. Variations of one request are permutations of one operation and
never a second call site; the `.bpd`'s header comment records which were collapsed and why.

**`BP06.03` to `BP06.07` were drawn in BP05 until 2026-10-10** and moved here on the Software
Architect's ruling, so that the measure and the commitment are one process each. They are minted
here as new call sites; `BP05.06`–`BP05.10` are retired there and never reused.
